How we researched. We interviewed three friends who had been to festivals or concerts and asked each of them the same questions: past experiences, how they find places and friends, and then their reaction to our prototype. Three interviews are useful early evidence for spotting problems and shaping priorities, but they do not represent all festivalgoers. Our interviewees know us and saw a prototype, so they may be more positive than strangers. That is why we also list what we still need to test.

Interviewee 3 described searching for friends and said a map with live location would help in exactly those situations. These are three accounts, not statistics.

What we changed. The research gave us a simple chain from finding to design response. Confusing maps led us to prioritize a clear map with place filters. Difficulty finding friends led to friend location sharing. Queuing and then hitting a sold-out item led to showing live food availability. The first two are already in the prototype; availability is the planned next step. Offline use and set-time alerts came from the same interviews and are covered in the improvements section.

Feedback and three improvements. Offline map: Interviewee 2 finds things complicated without Wi-Fi, so the map would be saved on the phone before signal drops. Live availability: Interviewee 1 described queuing and then finding food sold out, so items would show in stock, low or sold out next to the price. Set-time alerts: Interviewee 2 wants to know when an artist is about to start, so the app would alert you before a chosen set. None of these existed in the first prototype.

Value and feasibility. For attendees we expect less time searching, easier meetups, better food choices and fewer missed moments. These are hypotheses, not measured results. Behind the scenes, offline maps need venue data saved before signal drops; stock status needs timely updates from vendors; alerts need accurate schedules and a way to handle changes; queue estimates need to be trustworthy, and should show when they were last updated. Each one carries an assumption we still need to test.
